Keywords:                                                   Microwave radar has been widely utilized in structural displacement monitoring due to its long-range capabil­
Structural displacement measurement                         ities and strong environmental adaptability. Among them, millimeter-wave radar has emerged as a key tech­
Millimeter-wave radar interferometry                        nology for non-contact displacement sensing. Its short wavelength offers enhanced measurement resolution.
Adaptive phase unwrapping
                                                            However, the short wavelength also leads to phase wrapping issues during large structural movements, which
                                                            can lead to inaccurate displacement measurement. To overcome this challenge, this study proposes an improved
                                                            millimeter-wave radar interferometry technique for structural displacement measurement. A novel multi-chirp-
                                                            based algorithm is developed to adaptively recover phase wrapped signals beyond phase limits. The algorithm
                                                            first estimates the phase change rate using multi-chirps at each time step. It then predicts the phase for the next
                                                            time step, providing a reference phase range for phase unwrapping. Unlike conventional algorithms that rely on
                                                            empirical thresholds or additional sensors, the proposed algorithm operates autonomously. This feature makes it
                                                            well-suited for structural displacement monitoring in field conditions. Laboratory tests show the algorithm’s
                                                            accuracy in estimating large displacements (up to 5 cm), reducing root mean square errors by 98.80 % across
                                                            various excitations. A field test on a footbridge further confirms its reliability, with a displacement estimation
                                                            error of approximately 0.04 mm. These results demonstrate the potential of the proposed technique for high-
                                                            precision structural displacement monitoring in real-world applications.

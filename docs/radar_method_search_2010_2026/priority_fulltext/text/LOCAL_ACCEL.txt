Improved structural acceleration estimation using low-cost MEMS
accelerometer and FMCW millimeter-wave radar
Jigu Lee b , Kiyoung Kim b , Daurl Kim b, Zhanxiong Ma a,* , Hoon Sohn b,*
a
    College of Civil Engineering and Mechanics, Lanzhou University, Lanzhou, Gansu, China
b
    Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering, Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology, Daejeon, South Korea




A R T I C L E I N F O                                       A B S T R A C T

Keywords:                                                   Accurate acceleration is fundamental to structural health monitoring. However, microelectromechanical system
Structural acceleration measurement                         (MEMS) accelerometers, particularly low-cost accelerometers, suffer from low-frequency drift. To address this
Accelerometer                                               limitation, this study developed an integrated sensing system and fusion technique that estimates the structural
Millimeter-wave radar
                                                            acceleration by integrating a frequency-modulated continuous-wave (FMCW) millimeter-wave radar with a low-
Data fusion
                                                            cost MEMS accelerometer and an on-board microprocessor. Acceleration data were reconstructed using a finite
                                                            impulse response complementary filter that combines low-frequency components from the radar with high-
                                                            frequency components from the accelerometer. The technique and system were validated in the laboratory
                                                            under excitations at 0.1 Hz and 3.0 Hz, where the estimated acceleration closely aligned with the laser Doppler
                                                            vibrometer (LDV)-based results in both frequency and time domains, while suppressing accelerometer drifts and
                                                            radar noises. The robustness of the proposed system was validated employing a dynamic loading test on a
                                                            highway bridge. Relative to the MEMS accelerometer, the root-mean-square error was reduced by 85.4 % (20
                                                            km/h), 82.5 % (50 km/h), and 64.5 % (80 km/h) in the DC-2 Hz band. These results demonstrate that the
                                                            proposed technique yields drift-free and noise-resilient acceleration, making it suitable for large-scale
                                                            applications.
